Adults need between seven and nine hours of sleep per night. Consistently sleeping less than six hours is linked to impaired memory and a weaker immune response.

Caffeine has a half-life of about five hours, so a coffee drunk at four in the afternoon still leaves roughly half its caffeine in the body at nine at night. Many sleep specialists suggest avoiding caffeine after midday.

A cool, dark bedroom helps. The ideal room temperature for most people is around eighteen degrees Celsius. Screens emit blue light that can delay the release of melatonin, the hormone that signals bedtime.
