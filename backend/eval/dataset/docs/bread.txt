A sourdough starter is a living culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria kept in a mixture of flour and water. It must be fed regularly, usually once a day at room temperature, or once a week if stored in the refrigerator.

The basic dough uses flour, water and salt only. Bakers typically use a hydration of seventy percent, meaning seventy grams of water for every hundred grams of flour. Higher hydration gives a more open crumb but a stickier dough that is harder to shape.

After a long fermentation of about eight hours the loaf is shaped and rested overnight in the refrigerator. It is baked in a preheated Dutch oven at 250 degrees Celsius, first with the lid on to trap steam and then uncovered until the crust turns deep golden brown.
