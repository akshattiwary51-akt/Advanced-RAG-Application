An index fund holds all the stocks in a market index, so an investor owns a small slice of hundreds of companies at once. Because there is no manager picking stocks, annual fees are often below 0.2 percent.

Compound growth means returns earn their own returns. At an average annual return of seven percent, money roughly doubles every ten years. Starting early matters more than the exact amount invested.

An emergency fund of three to six months of living expenses should be kept in a savings account before investing. This prevents having to sell investments at a loss when an unexpected bill arrives.
